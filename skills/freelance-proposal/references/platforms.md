# Platform playbooks

Read only the section for the platform the user named in Step 0.

Each platform has a different reader in a different state of mind. Upwork and Fiverr readers are
drowning and hostile; Behance and email readers already chose to look at you. Writing the same
letter for all four is why most proposals underperform.

---

## Upwork

**The reader:** a client who posted a job and got 20-50 applications within hours, most of them
AI-written. They are annoyed, skimming, and will decide in seconds.

**The preview is everything.** Upwork's proposal list shows only the first ~225 characters of the
cover letter. That fragment is the entire basis on which the proposal gets opened. Write it last,
rewrite it three times, and count the characters. If it runs long, cut adjectives before you cut
substance.

**Length:** 180-260 words total, longer than the other platforms on purpose. Upwork letters use the
problem-first structure in SKILL.md Step 4 (*Upwork structure*): understanding, the problem as we
define it, the solution, optional experience plus the Expert-Vetted line on cold applications, one
question. The extra words go to thinking through the problem, not to credentials. Keep it
scannable: short paragraphs with white space between them.

**Links:** `mehmet-turan.com` only. Never `35milimetre.com`; it carries contact details and Upwork
prohibits routing clients off-platform. One link, inside the body.

**Video:** a Loom (or similar) link in the proposal body is normal practice and works well. Put it
after the observation, not before; the video earns its click from the text, not the other way round.

**Timing beats everything else you can control.** Apply within the first three hours of a post
going live, ideally the first hour. After 24 hours the client has usually stopped opening new
proposals; after 48 the job is often filled and just not closed. When a brief arrives that is
clearly days old, say so in the go/no-go read.

**Reading the client:** payment verified, total spend, hire rate versus posts, and the client's own
star rating all matter. A client with 100 posts and 10 hires is a connect furnace. A client rated
3.0 across 12 reviews is telling you they are difficult; 12 freelancers already said so.

**A short, plain brief is a good sign.** It usually means a real person, ready to hire, with no time
to waffle. Reply just as short; matching their energy reads as respect. A long, polished, structured
brief is often AI-generated and correlates with a client who is still shopping.

**Boosting** is a real lever on a job worth winning: it puts the proposal in the client's first
screen during the two-to-three hour window when they are actively reading. It is also a bidding war,
so it is worth it on the high-value jobs and wasted on the rest. Mention it in the go/no-go read only
when the job looks genuinely strong.

**Pricing:** Mehmet prefers fixed price with milestones over hourly. Frame it as "I'd rather give
you a fixed quote once I understand the scope" than quote an hourly rate.

**Output:** include the PREVIEW CHECK block.

## Fiverr

**The reader:** a buyer who posted a Brief and is receiving personalised offers from matched
sellers. Less crowded than Upwork, but the offer mechanics do a lot of the talking, so the message
has to set up the offer rather than duplicate it.

**Before writing anything on Fiverr, run Step 1b and read `references/deal-sequencing.md`.** Fiverr
is where the 30-thread review was done, and it is the only platform where the skill asks Mehmet
whether to send a qualifying message or a full quote. The dominant failure mode here is a good, long
offer landing before the buyer has decided their own scope or budget.

**Mechanics:** Briefs stay open for a seller to answer for **72 hours**. The response is an *offer*
plus an *introduction message*. The offer itself carries the payment structure; pick deliberately:

- **Single payment** — one deliverable, one fee. The default.
- **Milestones** — up to 5 steps. Right for multi-phase work (concept → render → finishing), and it
  de-risks a first job with a new client.
- **Subscription** — recurring monthly. Right when the brief hints at ongoing volume (a brand that
  shoots every season, a shop adding SKUs monthly). Worth proposing when the signal is there;
  recurring work is worth far more than a one-off at the same rate.
- **Hourly** — avoid; Mehmet prefers fixed.

Fiverr custom offers are bounded by $5-$20,000, 1-90 days delivery, and 0-20 or unlimited revisions.
Keep the recommended range and delivery window inside those bands.

**Length:** 100-150 words. Shorter than Upwork. The offer panel already states scope, price, delivery
and revisions, so the message should not repeat them; it should give the buyer a reason to trust the
person behind the offer.

**Links:** Fiverr's terms prohibit links that route traffic off-platform. `mehmet-turan.com` is the
one Mehmet uses because it carries no contact details; keep it to that single link, or drop it
entirely and lean on the gig gallery, which is the safer play. Never `35milimetre.com`.

**Video:** do not link to Loom or YouTube here. Upload the recording as an **attachment** to the
offer instead. Same script, same length; note in the output that it should be attached, not linked.

**Scope statement matters more here than anywhere else.** Fiverr buyers treat the delivered file as
the whole of the agreement, and revision expectations are the most common source of friction. The
scope statement should be pasted into the offer description before sending, not after.

**Output:** include the PREVIEW CHECK block; Fiverr's message list also truncates, and the discipline
of writing a strong first two lines is worth keeping regardless.

## Behance

**The reader:** someone who found Mehmet's actual work and liked it enough to write. This is the
warmest inbound there is. They have already seen the portfolio; re-selling it wastes the goodwill.

**35milimetre is the identity here.** Name the studio directly, link `35milimetre.com`, and reference
the relevant project on the Behance profile or the case studies and articles at
`35milimetre.com/news` where they genuinely fit. The contact-detail restriction does not apply.

**Different psychology:** they are not comparing forty applicants. They are deciding whether the
person behind the work is someone they want to talk to. So this letter can be warmer and slightly
more personal, and it should go straight to their project rather than to credentials.

Say something specific about *why their project is interesting* or what you noticed in their brief.
Then the diagnosis, then the opinion, then the question. Mention the studio shape honestly: three
people, plus specialists when a job needs them; the client works with the person doing the work.

**Length:** 130-200 words. Slightly longer is fine because the reader is already engaged.

**Mechanics:** Behance freelance inquiries convert into a formal proposal, and payment runs through
Stripe or PayPal, so this behaves more like a direct client engagement than a marketplace bid. That
means the scope statement and revision policy are doing real contractual work; write them carefully.

**Video:** a linked video is fine and welcome here.

**Output:** replace PREVIEW CHECK with a one-line note on the opening line, since Behance's inbox
preview is short but the reader is already committed.

## LinkedIn / email / direct

**The reader:** someone who did not ask to hear from you, or a warm contact continuing a thread.
Either way the inbox is the competition, not other freelancers.

**The subject line is the preview.** Same job as Upwork's 225 characters. Specific, lowercase-ish,
no marketing language, six to nine words. It should name their thing, not yours.
Good: *"the pouch shots on your new SKUs"*, *"quick thought on the Klar bottle renders"*.
Bad: *"Post-Production Services for Your Brand"*, *"Partnership Opportunity"*.

**Length:** 90-140 words. Assume it's read on a phone, in a hurry, between other mail.

**Links:** `35milimetre.com` is fine. One link only. No attachments on a first cold email; they hurt
deliverability and nobody opens them from a stranger.

**Structure:** one line naming the specific thing you noticed about their work; one line of what
you'd do about it; one line of proof; one easy question. Then a **PS line** — people who skim the
body and ignore the links still read the PS, so put the single most concrete or most human thing
there (a relevant project, a specific offer of a look at one image, an availability note).

**Video:** link it, and mention what's in it in six words so the click has a reason.

**Cold outreach honesty:** no fake familiarity, no "I've been following your work for years" unless
it's true, no invented mutual connections. The whole advantage here is that a real person looked at
their images; that is enough.

**Output:** replace PREVIEW CHECK with the SUBJECT LINE, and offer two alternatives so Mehmet can
pick.

## Quick comparison

| | Upwork | Fiverr | Behance | LinkedIn / email |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reader state | Drowning, hostile | Comparing offers | Already interested | Not expecting you |
| Words | 120-180 | 100-150 | 130-200 | 90-140 |
| The preview | First 225 chars | First 2 lines | Opening line | Subject line |
| Portfolio link | mehmet-turan.com | mehmet-turan.com (or none) | 35milimetre.com | 35milimetre.com |
| Studio name | Sign-off only | Sign-off only | Lead with it | Lead with it |
| Video | Link it | Attach it | Link it | Link it |
| Urgency | Apply within 3 hrs | 72-hour window | No rush | No rush |
| Qualify/quote step | No | **Yes, ask Mehmet** | No | No |
| Closing | One easy question | One easy question | One easy question | One easy question + PS |
| Call as CTA | Second option only | Second option only | Second option only | Second option only |
| Follow-up drafted | Yes, 4-7 days | Yes, 4-7 days | Yes, 5-7 days | Yes, 4-7 days |
