assume experienced commercial work for brand clients; they are not commodity freelance rates.

## How to present the number

**Lead with a first stage, not a range.** A wide range signals uncertainty to a buyer who is still
deciding, and a full-scope number asks for a big commitment before they have seen anything; both
correlate with silence in the archived-thread review. Structure it as:

> First stage: one [hero image / product build / test clip], **$X**, delivered by [date]. Once
> approved, the same setup runs across the remaining [quantity]; indicative **$Y-$Z** for the full
> set.

The pilot ladder in `references/deal-sequencing.md` says what "first stage" means for each service
type and what it should cost.

**Then say what moves the rollout number.** Name the two or three variables that decide where it
lands: number of deliverables, whether usable assets exist, background complexity, revision rounds,
deadline pressure. A range is fine *here*, attached to a defined scope, because by this point it is
describing variables rather than confessing uncertainty.

**Fixed price, not hourly.** Mehmet prefers project fees. Where a job could go either way, the
framing is: *"I'd rather give you a fixed quote once I understand the scope."* It sounds more
confident than an hourly rate and it protects both sides.

**Volume changes the per-unit price, and saying so is a selling point.** Twelve images in one recipe
costs less per image than twelve one-offs, because the first one carries the setup. Naming that
honestly makes the quote feel considered rather than plucked.

**Don't shave the number to match a low budget.** If a brief signals they want the cheapest option,
the honest read is that it's a mismatch, and saying so in the go/no-go is more useful to Mehmet than
a discounted quote he'll resent. There is always someone cheaper; competing there is a losing game
for a senior specialist.

**When they say it's too expensive, give paths, not explanations.** A price objection is usually a
real constraint. Justifying the cost does not give the buyer anything they can actually buy. Offer
full scope, a starter scope at one concrete component, or a paid consultation / production spec if
they will execute it elsewhere. See `references/deal-sequencing.md`.

**Recurring beats one-off at the same rate.** Where a brief hints at ongoing volume (seasonal
campaigns, monthly SKU additions, a growing catalogue), a monthly arrangement is worth proposing
once trust exists. Do not push it in a first cold proposal; it reads as presumptuous. Plant it
lightly ("if this goes well there's a sensible way to run it monthly") or save it for the call.

## Platform bands

**Fiverr custom offers:** $5-$20,000, 1-90 days delivery, 0-20 or unlimited revisions, up to 5
milestone steps. Keep the quoted range and delivery window inside those. Multi-phase work maps to
milestones; recurring work maps to a subscription offer.

**Upwork:** fixed-price with milestones is the default recommendation. Escrow funds per milestone,
which protects both parties and is worth explaining in a sentence to clients new to the platform.
