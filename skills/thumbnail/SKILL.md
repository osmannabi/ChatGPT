# /thumbnail: high-CTR thumbnail director

Built from two sources: the **Higgsfield "5 High-CTR Thumbnail Frameworks" playbook (Vol. 2, 2026)**
and 2026 platform specs and practitioner research (`references/research-2026.md`). The frameworks
decide *what the image is*; the 2026 rules decide *whether it survives the feed*.

## The one question every thumbnail answers

**What makes a viewer stop scrolling and need to know more?** The winning thumbnail raises a
question the title doesn't fully answer. Title and thumbnail are one package: they complement,
never repeat. If the thumb says "AI + REAL FOOTAGE", the title explains the how; it doesn't say
"AI + Real Footage" again.

## Workflow

1. **Intake (ask only what's missing).** Video topic and the single promise of the video; title
   (or draft); platform and placement (YouTube long-form, Shorts, Vimeo, Behance cover); available
   assets (footage frames, portraits, logos, product renders); whether the image will be shot,
   composited, AI-generated or pulled from footage; channel's existing look.
2. **Pick the framework** from the table below. Say why in one line.
3. **Sketch 3 variants** (different frameworks or different moments of the same one). Judge them
   side by side at feed size (about 360 px wide, and again at 168 px). Keep the one with the
   strongest unanswered question.
4. **Write the deliverable** (format at the end): concept, layout, text overlay, build brief, title pairing, test plan.
5. **Score it** against the 2026 checklist. Report pass/fail per line; fix fails before handing over.

## The five frameworks (Higgsfield playbook)

| # | Framework | Use when | The click lives in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Before/After** | The video transforms something: VFX, retouch, grade, AI upgrade, makeover | The gap between the halves: a process the viewer hasn't seen |
| 02 | **Mid-Action** | There is a peak physical or dramatic moment | An unresolved moment the viewer needs to see resolved |
| 03 | **Recognizable Logos** | The video is about specific tools, apps or brands | Tension between the logos and the claim beside them: leaves one question, *how?* |
| 04 | **3-Step Progression** | The process is the story, not just the result | The middle frame: a turning point that raises its own question |
| 05 | **Compelling Screenshot** | The footage itself is the spectacle (short film, cinematic piece, reel) | Authenticity: a real frame no composed graphic can fake |

### 01 Before/After
- Two states of **one subject** in one frame: raw start on one side, finished result on the other.
  The contrast sells before a word is read.
- Keep **pose, framing and lighting identical** across both halves so the result is the only variable.
- Verdict labels make it land in under a second: "VIDEO ✗ / CINEMA ✓". Short, opposing, with a mark.
- Strong for 35milimetre-type content: green screen → final composite, flat product → CGI hero, raw photo → retouched.

### 02 Mid-Action
- Freeze at the **peak**: body mid-fall, blast mid-detonation, face inches from the lens.
- **One action per thumbnail.** Motion blur, debris and expression all serve that single moment; anything else is noise.
- Re-staging or enhancing the frame after the shoot is fair, **as long as it stays true to what happens in the video.**

### 03 Recognizable Logos
- Logos are shortcuts: the viewer knows the tools in a fraction of a second, before reading.
- Hook = logo + claim tension ("$1280/day" between two app icons; an editing suite + "AI + REAL FOOTAGE").
- **Two or three big, clean logos maximum**, rendered large (3D app-icon style reads well).
- **Only combinations the video actually delivers.** A misleading pairing buys one click and costs credibility.

### 04 3-Step Progression
- Three frames, one journey: start, turning point, payoff. Read left to right.
- The **middle beat must raise its own question**; "halfway done" has no pull.
- Same subject, same angle; the whole arc lands in under a second with no caption needed.
- Hardest to read small: test at 168 px wide before committing; use only on clean, simple subjects.

### 05 Compelling Screenshot
- Scrub the footage for the frame whose **energy matches the title's promise**: the whole video compressed into one image.
- Choose a frame **close to how the video opens**, so the first seconds confirm the promise (protects retention, which is what YouTube's test scores).
- Pair with minimal branding: series badge plus one-word title with an accent bar (the "Higgsfield Originals" pattern).

## 2026 rules (apply to every framework)

### Spec
- **YouTube long-form:** 16:9, upload **3840x2160** (YouTube's current recommendation; 1280x720 still
  accepted), minimum 640 px wide. JPG or PNG. Up to **50 MB from desktop, 2 MB from mobile** upload.
- **Shorts:** 9:16, 2160x3840; the frame is often chosen from the video, so compose the opening frame as a thumbnail.
- Work in sRGB; export JPG at high quality for photographic thumbs, PNG for flat graphic ones.

### Safe zones
- Keep face, text and logos inside the **center ~86% x 86%** (1100x620 at 1280x720; scale x3 at 4K),
  roughly **8% horizontal and 10% vertical** margins.
- **Bottom-right corner is dead:** duration badge (wider on long videos, e.g. 1:23:45). Never put text or a face there.
- **Bottom edge:** red watch-progress bar on partly watched videos.
- Mobile feeds and some surfaces crop toward 4:3: keep the key element off the far left and right edges.

### Readability (the postage-stamp test)
- **0-3 words** of overlay text; a sentence belongs in the title. Text should read at 168 px wide.
- Heavy condensed sans, all caps or tight title case, with a stroke, shadow or color block behind it.
- **One focal point.** Three or fewer distinct elements total (e.g. face + object + word).
- High value contrast between subject and background; check in grayscale. Saturated accent (the
  playbook's acid lime on dark is a good example) used once, not everywhere.
- Large face: rule of thumb, a third or more of the frame height when a person is the subject; eyes sharp.

### Faces and authenticity ("proof of human")
- Expressive faces lift CTR, but in 2026 **genuine micro-expressions** (focus, disbelief, real
  laughter) beat the exaggerated mouth-agape cliché.
- Feeds are flooded with generic AI imagery and viewers scroll past it. If the image is AI-generated
  or AI-enhanced: keep real skin texture and pores, plausible light direction, natural eyes and hands;
  avoid plastic skin, impossible rim light, uncanny symmetry. Ground it with a real photo or real
  footage frame wherever possible; retouch, don't replace.

### Honesty
- The thumbnail must be something the video **delivers in its first moments**. Misleading packaging
  wins clicks and loses watch time, and watch time is what YouTube's test and recommendation reward.
  It can also breach YouTube's misleading-metadata policy.

### Consistency
- A recognizable channel system (fixed font, accent color, badge position, framing habit) raises CTR
  from returning viewers. Vary the image, not the system.

### Testing
- Use YouTube **Test & Compare**: up to **3 variants** of thumbnail, title, or title+thumbnail
  combos; desktop Studio only; runs up to **2 weeks**; winner chosen by **watch time share**, not CTR.
  Results come back as Winner / Performed same / Inconclusive.
- Make variants genuinely different (different framework or moment), not a color tweak.
- Rough expectations (2026 blog data, not YouTube figures): CTR 2-10% by niche; average channels 3-4%, top creators 5-10%. Judge against
  your own channel's baseline, not blog averages.

## Building the image

- **Shoot or composite (preferred for credibility):** list the plate(s), the subject pose and
  expression, lens (35-50mm for faces; avoid wide-angle distortion unless intended), light direction
  and a separation light, plus the retouch passes (dodge/burn for contrast, eye and catchlight
  enhancement, background simplification, local saturation).
- **AI generation:** write the prompt per model; for GPT Image 2 use the `gpt-image-2-director` skill,
  for Higgsfield/Nano Banana keep the same subject reference across variants. Always state 16:9,
  leave negative space where the text goes, and generate **without** text, then set the type in
  Photoshop/Figma so it's crisp and editable.
- **Text and logos** go on last as vector or high-res layers; never let a generator draw logos.

## Deliverable format

```
FRAMEWORK: <name> — <one-line reason>
QUESTION IT RAISES: <what the viewer needs answered>
TITLE PAIRING: <title> (complements, does not repeat the overlay)

LAYOUT (16:9):
- Focal subject: <what, where, size>
- Secondary element: <logo / object / second state>
- Text overlay: "<0-3 words>" — <font style, color, position>, outside bottom-right
- Background: <simplified, value contrast note>

BUILD BRIEF: <shoot/composite steps or AI prompt + retouch passes>

VARIANTS FOR TEST & COMPARE:
A: <...>  B: <...>  C: <...>

CHECKLIST: <table, pass/fail>
```

## 2026 checklist

| # | Check |
|---|---|
| 1 | One framework, one focal point, ≤ 3 elements |
| 2 | Raises a question the title doesn't answer; title doesn't repeat the overlay |
| 3 | 0-3 words, readable at 168 px wide; grayscale contrast holds |
| 4 | Nothing important in the bottom-right badge zone or bottom edge; key content inside ~8%/10% margins |
| 5 | Face (if any) large, sharp eyes, genuine expression; no uncanny AI tells |
| 6 | Only logos/claims the video delivers; matches the opening seconds |
| 7 | 3840x2160 (or ≥ 1280x720), 16:9, JPG/PNG, under 2 MB if uploading from mobile |
| 8 | Fits the channel's visual system |
| 9 | 2-3 genuinely different variants ready for Test & Compare |
